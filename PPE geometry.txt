### Design cues for your Blender models
- **Scale relationships** are nearly linear: each step (~×5 then ×4) multiplies the linear geometry by ~1.5× each time.  
  So you can model one module and scale it by ×1.5 for regional, ÷1.5 for micro to keep proportions.
- **Building height (10 m)** stays constant; only floor area and equipment count grow.
- **Most large items** (sand storage, NaCl pile, absorbers) push near the roof height already, giving a strong vertical anchor inside the hall.
- **Outdoor reflection field** dominates land use visually—everything else is a fraction of that.

---

### Recommended Blender workflow
1. Import these primitives as meta objects:  
   `cylinder`, `box`, `plane`, `cone`, `sphere-capped cylinder`.  
2. Parent each subsystem to an *Empty* named by service for easy scaling.  
3. For multi‑module scaling, duplicate grouped empties with scale factors (×0.67 micro, ×1 base, ×1.5 regional).  
4. Keep solar field, sand bed, and NaCl pile as external collection in the scene for flexible placement.

---

### What you’ll see
- The **1 t/day** model will look like a lab‑sized skid housed in a double‑garage‑scale building.  
- The **5 t/day** “warehouse” will look like a compact community plant roughly one supermarket in footprint.  
- The **20 t/day** “regional” version scales into a robust light‑industrial complex comparable to a small logistics warehouse with an adjacent solar‑thermal yard.

That triple set will give reviewers a tangible sense of scale and make your later Phase 4 proposal visuals compelling and easy to follow.
